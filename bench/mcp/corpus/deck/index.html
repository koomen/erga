Tidewater Robotics

Hull-cleaning robots for working harbours · Seed round, November

The problem

  • Barnacles and weed on a ship's hull raise fuel use by up to 10%.
  • Cleaning today means divers or a dry dock: slow, costly and dangerous.
  • New rules in the EU and Australia limit in-water cleaning that releases debris.

Our robot

  • A magnetic crawler that cleans a hull while the ship is loading in port.
  • It captures 98% of the debris it removes, so ports allow it.
  • One operator on the quay runs two robots from a tablet.

Business model

  • Ports pay a yearly licence for each robot stationed on their quay.
  • Shipping lines pay per clean, booked with their berth.
  • Gross margin on a clean is 64% today, and 75% at two robots per port.

Market

  • About 60,000 merchant ships over 5,000 tonnes trade worldwide.
  • Each needs two to four cleanings a year to keep its fuel bill down.
  • We start with the 40 busiest container ports, where ships wait longest.

Team

  • Maren Olsen, CEO: ran port operations at a North Sea terminal for eight years.
  • David Okafor, CTO: built underwater inspection drones for offshore wind.
  • Lucía Gómez, head of sales: sold berth software to 30 ports in Europe.

The ask

  • We are raising $6M to build 20 robots and open five more ports.
  • Half of the round goes to robots, a third to the team, the rest to certification.
  • The round gives us 24 months of runway.

Roadmap

  • Next quarter: second-generation robot with twice the cleaning speed.
  • Next year: Rotterdam, Singapore and Long Beach.
  • In two years: inspection reports for insurers from the same robot.
