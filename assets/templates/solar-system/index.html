A tour in nine steps

The solar system

One star and eight planets that go around it. Step forward to visit each planet, from the Sun outwards. (Sizes, distances and speeds are not to scale.)

Planet 1

Mercury

The smallest planet, and the closest to the Sun. It has almost no air, so its days are hot enough to melt lead and its nights are far below freezing.

  • Year 88 days
  • Moons 0

Planet 2

Venus

Thick clouds of sulfuric acid trap the heat, which makes Venus the hottest planet. It turns backwards, so the Sun rises in the west.

  • Year 225 days
  • Moons 0

Planet 3

Earth

Our home, and the only place we know with life. Oceans of liquid water cover about seven tenths of its surface.

  • Year 365 days
  • Moons 1

Planet 4

Mars

Iron oxide dust makes the surface red. Mars has the tallest volcano we know, Olympus Mons, about two and a half times as high as Everest.

  • Year 687 days
  • Moons 2

Planet 5

Jupiter

A giant of gas, more than twice as massive as all the other planets together. Its Great Red Spot is a storm wider than Earth.

  • Year 12 years
  • Moons 95

Planet 6

Saturn

Its rings are billions of pieces of ice and rock. The planet is so light for its size that it would float in a large enough bath.

  • Year 29 years
  • Moons 146

Planet 7

Uranus

An ice giant that lies on its side, perhaps after a collision long ago. Each pole has about 42 years of sun, then 42 years of dark.

  • Year 84 years
  • Moons 28

Planet 8

Neptune

The farthest planet, dark, cold and very windy: its storms blow at more than 2,000 km an hour. Sunlight takes about four hours to get there.

  • Year 165 years
  • Moons 16
